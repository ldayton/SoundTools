JEWISH YEMENITE MUSIC AND MODAL SCALE PRACTICE
Research findings and recommendations for DoReGo
Prepared for Lily — 21 November 2026

PURPOSE AND RECOMMENDATION

DoReGo can usefully add short modal exercises informed by Jewish Yemenite
music, alongside its existing Rast, Bayati and Hijaz octave exercises.

The recommended first release is:

  1. Rast pentachord: five notes spanning a perfect fifth.
  2. Rast tetrachord: four notes spanning a perfect fourth.
  3. Bayati tetrachord and pentachord.
  4. Sikah trichord: three notes spanning a neutral third.

The first two have especially clear support in documented Jewish Yemenite
recordings. Bayati and Sikah are valuable additions to general modal practice;
the research completed here does not establish them as named Jewish Yemenite
teaching categories.

Use musical names in the exercise selector and explain cultural connections
in source notes. Do not introduce a single preset called "the Yemenite scale"
or "the Jewish Yemenite scale."

This report specifies proposed behavior. No product code has been changed for
this report, and no implementation or performance-tuning validation is claimed.


1. EVIDENCE: DOCUMENTED JEWISH YEMENITE PITCH COLLECTIONS

Jean Lambert's 2023 study analyzes historical recordings made by Hans Helfritz
in 1930–31. The Jewish examples below are women from Dhamar. Reference letters
are conventional, not a statement of the recordings' absolute pitch. [1]

ID   Example / context                  Published pitch inventory
84   Ya zayn ya zayn                    A B-half-flat C D E F
85   Ala ya ra'd ya hannan              G A Bdb C
86   Nazalt as-sayla; bridal procession C D E-half-flat F G
91   Funeral song                      C D E-half-flat F G
92   Ala bi-llah amana; wedding         C D E-half-flat F G

Recording 85 prints "Bdb"; verify its accidental against the audio before use.
Recordings 91 and 92 use the same melody in different social contexts. [1]

Our provisional quarter-tone normalization, measured from the lowest listed
pitch, is:

84: 0, 150, 300, 500, 700, 800 cents
85: 0, 200, 350, 500 cents IF Bdb means B-half-flat
86, 91, 92: 0, 200, 350, 500, 700 cents

These are calculated reference values, not measurements. A pitch inventory
alone does not establish a tonic. Lambert calls the analysis preliminary and
warns against an unqualified application of maqam theory. [1]


2. WHAT THE OTHER SOURCES ADD

Marilyn Herman's 1996 study describes diwan melodies organized around single
tetrachords, conjunct tetrachords sharing a note, and disjunct tetrachords.
Examples include Tsur Menati and Sar Ha-Memuneh. Her discussion of women's
repertoires includes pentatonic collections and melodies revolving around
small nuclei. In some conjunct structures, the final note is the junction.
This is evidence for examining internal melodic centers rather than assuming
the lowest pitch is always home. [2]

The exact staff examples in Herman's PDF were not visually verified during
this investigation. No numeric presets have been extracted from those staff
examples. Their transcription remains follow-up work.

The Smithsonian's archival diwan release distinguishes synagogue chant,
men's devotional poetry singing at social celebrations, and women's orally
transmitted songs. Its documentation also describes the reuse of tunes for
different poems and changes of melody within a poem. A text title therefore
does not uniquely identify a scale or tune. [3]

For the general Arabic teaching framework, MaqamWorld identifies Sikah as a
three-note jins and Bayati as a four-note jins. Its Rast presentation uses
five notes. A jins is a melodic building block; a full maqam includes further
melodic behavior and cannot be reduced to an interval array. [4–6]


3. PROPOSED EXERCISE DEFINITIONS

All formulas below are proposed quarter-tone reference exercises. One semitone
is 100 cents. A half-flat lowers a written natural by 50 cents in this model.
These exact values are convenient reference tuning, not a claim that singers
use fixed 50-cent increments.

Name                  Semitone offsets        Adjacent steps, cents
Rast tetrachord       0 2 3.5 5                200 150 150
Rast pentachord       0 2 3.5 5 7              200 150 150 200
Bayati tetrachord     0 1.5 3 5                150 150 200
Bayati pentachord     0 1.5 3 5 7              150 150 200 200
Sikah trichord        0 1.5 3.5                150 200

Reference spellings:

Rast:   C D E-half-flat F [G]
Bayati: D E-half-flat F G [A]
Sikah:  E-half-flat F G

The bracketed final note distinguishes the pentachord from the tetrachord.
The formulas are derived practice definitions using the reference convention;
they are not transcriptions of five specific traditional performances.

Educational purpose:

Rast makes the neutral third above home audible. Bayati moves the neutral
interval to the second degree. Sikah practices a neutral second followed by
a whole tone, with a small range appropriate for focused intonation work.

Terminology matters. Call the four-note Rast fragment "Rast tetrachord."
Call the three-note exercise "Sikah trichord" or "Jins Sikah," not a complete
performance of Maqam Sikah. The extended Bayati pentachord is an exercise
extension; the core Bayati jins remains the four-note unit.

Sikah's conventional E-half-flat spelling does not require an absolute
quarter-tone starting pitch in every transposition. Starting on C and using
offsets 0, 150, 350 cents preserves the proposed interval relationships.


4. CANDIDATES TO HOLD FOR FURTHER RESEARCH

A. Six-note collection from recording 84

The normalized inventory 0, 150, 300, 500, 700, 800 is a promising compact
exercise. Before making it a cultural preset, establish the melodic home,
the actual note usage, and whether the inventory changes during the recording.
Do not infer the tonic solely by sorting the pitches. Do not label it Bayati
just because its first four normalized notes fit the Bayati formula.

B. Full Sikah octave

Useful in a broader maqam expansion, but it requires an explicitly selected
upper structure and naming convention. The trichord does not uniquely specify
the upper part of a maqam. Do not extend the three-note formula mechanically
and imply that every Sikah-family melody follows that extension. [7]

C. A broader Yemeni Bayati upper variant

The earlier discussion proposed an upper half-flat sixth relative to D,
yielding D E-half-flat F G A B-half-flat C. Treat this as a separately sourced
broader Yemeni theoretical candidate. It has not been established here as a
Jewish Yemenite preset. DoReGo's existing Bayati array is not thereby proven
wrong and should not be silently replaced.

D. Pentatonic Jewish Yemenite examples

The literature supports investigating them, but "pentatonic" means five-note
and does not supply a unique formula. Do not substitute a familiar major or
minor pentatonic scale without verifying a particular example.


5. RECOMMENDED FIRST-RELEASE EXPERIENCE

Keep the main interaction simple:

  Collection: Rast / Bayati / Sikah
  Extent: the supported named fragment or existing octave form
  Starting pitch: the user's comfortable pitch
  Direction: ascending / descending / ascending and descending
  Tonic drone: optional

Show only extents with authored definitions. Rast can offer four notes, five
notes and its existing octave form. Bayati can offer the same sizes. Sikah
initially offers its three-note fragment. An arbitrary "3–6 notes" control
would imply more validated combinations than this research currently supplies.

Suggested explanatory copy:

  "Practice a short modal pitch collection. Quarter-tone reference tuning."

For the Rast pentachord, an expandable source note can say:

  "This pitch collection is documented in Jewish women's songs from Dhamar,
   Yemen. This exercise uses an approximate reference tuning."

Put research detail behind the explanation, rather than in the required flow.
Keep a source-specific example distinct from the general collection it uses.

First-release ascending/descending practice may use the collection's first
note as home. That is an authored exercise choice. Do not claim that this
assigns a verified tonic to every historical recording sharing those pitches.

Do not automatically add an octave above a short fragment. A three-note Sikah
exercise should remain three notes unless the user selects a separately
authored extension.

For a return pattern, an explicit example is:

  Rast pentachord: 1 2 3 4 5 4 3 2 1
  Bayati tetrachord: 1 2 3 4 3 2 1
  Sikah trichord: 1 2 3 2 1

Repeated peaks, repeated home notes and durations should follow DoReGo's
established controls. These paths are teaching proposals, not traditional
Yemenite melodic formulas.


6. DATA AND GENERATION MODEL

The inspected working copy already contains Rast, Bayati and Hijaz reference
tunings. Its maqamat generation constructs an octave and maps seven major
degrees to a tuning array. This design assumes seven available degree entries.
Adding a three- or four-element array alone would leave that assumption broken.

Relevant files inspected:

  apps/dorego/src/music.ts
  apps/dorego/src/note-spelling.ts
  apps/dorego/src/audio.ts
  apps/dorego/src/builder.tsx
  apps/dorego/tests/maqam.test.ts

The working tree was already being edited during this investigation. These
observations are a snapshot, not a claim about the committed or released app.

Recommended collection model:

  id: stable identifier, such as rast-pentachord
  name: display name
  offsetsCents: explicit ordered pitch values
  degreeSpellings: letters and accidentals relative to a reference spelling
  homeIndex: the authored exercise's reference center
  extent: explicit description of the supported range
  octaveEquivalent: explicit; never inferred from array length
  tuningModel: quarter-tone-reference initially
  sources: citations and evidence notes

Keep the exercise path separate from the collection. Represent a path as
indices into its pitches, then transpose the result. This supports three-,
four-, five- and six-note material without pretending all collections are
seven-degree octave scales.

Frequency conversion:

  frequency = referenceFrequency * 2^(offsetCents / 1200)

Alternatively, preserve the existing fractional MIDI representation through
playback. Do not round microtonal values to integer MIDI notes internally.

Future recording-derived material may need a homeIndex inside its range,
negative offsets below home, and no octave repetition. Prepare the model for
those possibilities without exposing unnecessary controls in the first release.


7. NOTATION, AUDIO AND COMPATIBILITY REQUIREMENTS

Notation:

  - Preserve intended degree letters through transposition.
  - Spell the neutral third as the altered third degree, even where another
    enharmonic label could represent the same reference frequency.
  - Distinguish a half-flat from a flat clearly in screen and export views.
  - Apply the same pitch and spelling rules to descending exercises.

Audio:

  - Preserve fractional pitches in live playback and supported audio exports.
  - Point the drone at the exercise's actual home frequency after transposition.
  - Keep Western starting chords suppressed for these exercises, consistent
    with the inspected maqamat generation behavior.
  - Keep reference tuning and performed regional intonation conceptually separate.

Compatibility:

  - Preserve existing saved Rast, Bayati and Hijaz settings.
  - Use explicit collection IDs and define how old settings map to octave forms.
  - Validate supported collection/extent combinations on restore and import.
  - Recheck range, duration, repeat and progression behavior for short collections.
  - Inspect any MIDI export path separately: conventional MIDI note numbers
    alone cannot encode these fractional pitches accurately.


8. VERIFICATION AND DELIVERY PLAN

Before implementation, identify all consumers of the existing tuning and
exercise contracts across app tests and root integration tests. Follow
docs/testing.md and the executable policy in scripts/ci/test-strategy.cjs.

Meaningful verification should cover:

  - Independent expected formulas for every newly authored collection.
  - Exact note counts and ranges, including no unwanted octave extension.
  - Both directions and returning paths.
  - Transposition preserving interval relationships and degree spelling.
  - Drone frequency matching the selected exercise home.
  - Persistence, import/export and migration of existing settings.
  - Browser selection and notation, including quarter-tone accidental glyphs.
  - Relevant offline/account consumers if their persisted contract changes.

Register new behavior in the executable suite policy. Before pushing any code,
read the current CI configuration, run every applicable local lane against the
exact commit, and run git diff --check. This report itself does not implement
behavior and has not triggered a code validation run.

Suggested delivery sequence:

Phase 1: Explicit short-collection generation, Rast pentachord and tetrachord.
Phase 2: Bayati fragments and Sikah trichord using the same model.
Phase 3: Source-specific six-note and pentatonic material after transcription.
Phase 4: Adjustable intonation and richer melodic paths, if practice needs them.

No phase should be marketed as a complete representation of Jewish Yemenite
music. The achievable first product is a useful set of accurately named scale
exercises with transparent evidence and tuning assumptions.


9. RESEARCH STILL NEEDED FOR CULTURAL PRESETS

  1. Listen to the historical examples and check printed accidentals.
  2. Establish phrase endings, repeated focal notes and likely tonal centers.
  3. Measure representative sustained pitches, separating ornament from targets
     and accounting for recording-speed uncertainty.
  4. Check complete performances for changing pitch inventories.
  5. Visually inspect Herman's notation examples before extracting formulas.
  6. Compare multiple performers of the same poem or tune.
  7. Review transcriptions with a specialist in Jewish Yemenite performance.
  8. Verify recording and transcription rights before distributing excerpts.

The archival study, general modal references and historical descriptions do
not replace those steps. No acoustic pitch measurements or specialist review
were completed in this investigation.
